DATA DESCRIPTION AND PREPROCESSING

This document describes the datasets and preprocessing steps adopted for
the analysis of Arctic Sea Ice Thickness (SIT) using machine learning
methods.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DATA DESCRIPTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The analysis integrates reanalysis, observational, hydrological, and
large-scale climate datasets to investigate the drivers of Arctic Sea
Ice Thickness variability.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 PAN-ARCTIC ICE-OCEAN MODELING AND ASSIMILATION SYSTEM (PIOMAS)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The Pan-Arctic Ice-Ocean Modeling and Assimilation System (PIOMAS) is a
reanalysis dataset providing daily and monthly estimates of Arctic Sea
Ice Thickness (SIT) starting from January 1978.

PIOMAS is based on a coupled sea-ice–ocean model that assimilates
observations of sea surface temperature, sea ice concentration, and sea
ice velocity. It is widely used for monitoring Arctic sea ice changes,
studying Arctic amplification, and validating numerical and statistical
sea ice models.

The dataset realistically reproduces the mean Pan-Arctic SIT, its
spatial variability, and the seasonal cycle, as well as long-term trends
in ice thickness and volume. A known limitation is the absence of direct
assimilation of in situ or satellite-derived ice thickness observations,
which can lead to overestimation of thin ice and underestimation of
thick ice.

PIOMAS data are provided on a generalized curvilinear grid with spatial
dimensions of 360 × 120, covering latitudes from 45°N to 90°N. In this
study, Sea Ice Thickness is used as the target variable, while other
PIOMAS variables are not considered.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 GLOBAL HISTORICAL CLIMATOLOGY NETWORK (GHCN)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The Global Historical Climatology Network (GHCN-Daily) is a comprehensive
archive of daily meteorological observations collected from more than
100,000 stations worldwide and subjected to rigorous quality control
procedures.

The dataset includes daily measurements of precipitation, snowfall,
snow depth, and minimum and maximum temperatures. Temporal coverage
extends from 1860 to 2024, although record length varies across stations.

Each station is uniquely identified by an ID and associated with
metadata describing its geographical location and elevation.

The variables selected for this study are
- Liquid precipitation (PRCP, mm)
- Solid precipitation (SNOW, mm)
- Snow depth (SNWD, mm)
- Minimum temperature (TMIN, tenths of °C)
- Maximum temperature (TMAX, tenths of °C)

Only stations located in Arctic and sub-Arctic regions are considered.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3 ARCTIC GREAT RIVERS OBSERVATORY (ARCTICGRO)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The Arctic Great Rivers Observatory (ArcticGRO) dataset provides daily
river discharge measurements (m³s) for major Arctic rivers, representing
freshwater input into the Arctic Ocean.

In this study, five rivers are selected Ob, Yenisei, Lena, Yukon, and
Mackenzie. For each river, runoff measurements are taken at the most
downstream available gauging station. The temporal coverage varies by
river, ranging from several decades to nearly a century.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4 NOAA TELECONNECTION INDICES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Teleconnection indices from NOAA describe large-scale climate patterns
that influence atmospheric and oceanic variability across distant
regions.

These indices are not explicitly included in physically-based climate
models due to their non-local and non-linear nature. However, machine
learning techniques are able to exploit such relationships, making
teleconnection indices valuable predictors for Arctic Sea Ice Thickness.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA PREPROCESSING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

This section describes the preprocessing steps applied to each dataset
prior to feature selection and machine learning modeling.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 PIOMAS SEA ICE THICKNESS PREPROCESSING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Sea Ice Thickness from PIOMAS is used as the target variable of the
analysis. The data are defined over time, latitude, and longitude on a
curvilinear grid.

To reduce spatial dimensionality and ensure computational feasibility,
the SIT field is clustered using the K-means algorithm. Five homogeneous
Arctic regions are identified, and each grid cell is assigned to one
cluster.

For each cluster, a regional mean SIT time series is computed by
averaging SIT over all grid cells belonging to the same region. This
approach preserves dominant spatial patterns while avoiding pixel-wise
modeling.

The resulting SIT time series exhibit strong seasonality and a clear
long-term declining trend. Regions characterized by thicker ice show
higher interannual variability, while thinner-ice regions display lower
variance and more gradual declines.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 GHCN METEOROLOGICAL DATA PREPROCESSING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Daily GHCN observations are first filtered to retain only selected
variables and stations located in Arctic and sub-Arctic regions.

Preprocessing steps include
- Use of quality-controlled data only
- Aggregation of daily observations to monthly resolution
- Computation of monthly means for temperature variables
- Computation of monthly totals for precipitation, snowfall, and snow
  depth
- Conversion of temperature values from tenths of °C to °C
- Removal of stations with excessive missing data

Each variable-station pair is encoded using a simplified naming
convention to facilitate feature selection and visualization.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 ARCTICGRO HYDROLOGICAL DATA PREPROCESSING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Daily discharge data from ArcticGRO are aggregated to monthly mean
discharge values to ensure temporal consistency with the SIT target.

Additional preprocessing steps include
- Removal of duplicated or inconsistent records
- Exclusion of months with insufficient daily data coverage
- Standardization of discharge units (m³s)

Each river discharge time series is associated with a unique flow
identifier and used as an independent predictor.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 NOAA TELECONNECTION DATA PREPROCESSING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Teleconnection indices are provided as monthly time series and therefore
do not require temporal aggregation.

Preprocessing steps include
- Selection of indices relevant to Arctic climate variability
- Temporal alignment with the common analysis period
- Removal of missing values at the beginning or end of the time series
- Standardization to zero mean and unit variance

Teleconnection indices are included as large-scale climate predictors,
allowing the machine learning models to capture non-linear and remote
climate influences on Arctic Sea Ice Thickness.
